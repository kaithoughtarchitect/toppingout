f-sketch

/f-sketchskill
Full pagesample, questions, links→
Does
Write down a rough idea and its open questions, before anything is decided.
When
When you have an idea worth writing down but you're not ready to decide anything about it yet.
Say it
capture this ideaspec this concept out

Not forDecision-complete work (f-blueprint), settling decisions (f-survey), a job's own plan (the foreman's PLAN.md), or single Logbook cards (f-card).

It's working if
  • It wrote exactly one file, foreman/ideas/IDEA-YYYY-MM-DD_<short-slug>.md, or placed it inside an existing idea directory and said so.
  • The file opens with a title, a date, a status saying the discussion is open, a banner saying it is not a plan of record and not executable, and one line on where it came from.
  • Settled points appear as A0… agreements and open questions as Q0…, with the options as they were discussed, not resolved.
  • Nothing in it was invented: no locked decision tables, no ordered tasks, no answers to questions you never answered.
  • foreman/blueprints/README.md has one new bullet for it in the Ideas section, naming what blocks promotion.
  • The report gives the path, a one-line summary, the counts of agreements, open questions and rejected options, and the blocking questions.
  • It opened no job, wrote no plan or code, and staged, committed and pushed nothing.

f-survey

/f-surveyskill
Full pagesample, questions, links→
Does
Turn a rough request into clear decisions, one round of questions at a time.
When
When a request is still vague or has open choices, before anyone writes the plan.
Say it
survey thisgroom thisthis is still vaguetighten this before we buildwhat am I missingsurvey this out loud

Not forThe site walk (the machine check at the start of a job, METHOD §2). Not for writing the plan (f-blueprint) or cutting it into tickets (f-schedule).

It's working if
  • Each round opens with the survey layout: your call, settled by the foreman, on site, next round.
  • "Your call" holds at most three questions, each with the recommended option first and starred.
  • Anything the code or the evidence can answer sits under "Settled by the foreman", with the evidence named, not as a question to you.
  • No question in a round depends on another question still open in the same round.
  • It stops asking once another answer would only tune implementation detail.
  • It ends with one self-contained commission block: in scope, out of scope, acceptance criteria, decisions with their reasons, the likely lane and the exact next action (/f-blueprint <topic>, or park it).
  • It created no job, plan, code or commit along the way.

f-blueprint

/f-blueprintskill
Full pagesample, questions, links→
Does
Write the full plan once every decision is made.
When
When every choice is settled and it's time to write the plan for the whole project.

Not forOpen decisions (f-survey), not for cutting into tickets (f-schedule), not for building.

It's working if
  • It wrote exactly one file, foreman/blueprints/ready/BLUEPRINT-YYYY-MM-DD_<short-slug>.md, with a status of ready or blocked-on-decision.
  • The plan names the head, the locked decisions (D0…), the verification points, the ordered tasks (T0…) and every assumption, HALT and outward item, with research and owner decisions listed as such, not hidden inside a task.
  • Every unresolved row carries a default if silent, or HALT until decided.
  • Each task names the paths it owns, its prerequisites, its outcome, what it traces to, and a verify step that uses a gate the repo already has.
  • The plan ends with a ## Plan check findings heading and an empty findings table, ready for /f-plancheck.
  • foreman/blueprints/README.md has a Ready entry for it, usually saying it needs /f-plancheck.
  • It implemented nothing, opened no job, did not run /f-plancheck, and staged, committed and pushed nothing.

f-plancheck

/f-plancheckskill
Full pagesample, questions, links→
Does
Have AI models from other companies read the plan fresh and point out problems.
When
After a plan is written and before it's split into jobs; also on a job's own plan before its second work order.
It's working if
  • Before picking seats, it prints who wrote the plan and which model family is excluded, so no seat comes from the author's own family.
  • On an attended run, it opens a one-shot picker in your browser and posts the picker's link in chat; nothing is dispatched until you have picked 1 to 5 seats.
  • Each seat gets the ticket's own words and checks every decision against them as well as against the code.
  • It reports the findings of the mechanical checks (paths, file:line citations, CLI flags, measurements, the Done-when block) as findings, without fixing them.
  • It writes findings.md in a fresh scratch folder: one block per defect, each with where, which seats raised it, the evidence, and an empty Disposition line.
  • Findings that change what is built sit in their own section, marked DO NOT ACT and left for your decision, with no suggested fix, and the report shows each of them to you in full.
  • The plan is untouched apart from one gate: plancheck line, which a plan in or headed for foreman/blueprints/ready/ always gets.
  • It staged, committed and pushed nothing.

04of 11

f-schedule

/f-scheduleskill
Full pagesample, questions, links→
Does
Split a big plan into jobs, in order, and draw the project board.
When
When a plan has passed the plan check and is ready to be split into jobs.
Say it
break this plan into ticketsdecompose this into jobsturn this plan into a project

Not forWriting the plan (f-blueprint), not for planning one ticket's job (the foreman's scoping + PLAN.md), not for board updates after that (f-board).

It's working if
  • On a plan without the gate: plancheck line, it stops and tells you to run /f-plancheck first. A findings row with a blank fate stops it too.
  • Before writing anything, it shows you the head, the job table, the decision jobs, any unmapped verification points or tasks, any disagreements, and the job it recommends first.
  • It writes nothing until you say "go" for the project.
  • Research and owner decisions are their own jobs, never hidden inside another: a question that ends in your answer is a decision job; research that produces a file is a build job and goes through a PR.
  • Every verification point is carried by at least one job, and every plan task maps to a job or to "not carried, on purpose" with its reason.
  • foreman/projects/<slug>/ holds PROJECT.md, one ticket file per job with the acceptance quoted and a "Why this job" that says in plain sentences what the job makes possible, never only its plan task, and a BOARD.json that render_board.py validated and rendered.
  • Each job on the board has a summary: one plain sentence saying what it builds, taken from the blueprint task's Outcome, with plan references after it in brackets, never a summary of references alone.
  • It committed nothing, and never deleted or rewrote the source plan.

f-inspect

/f-inspectskill
Full pagesample, questions, links→
Does
Have specialist reviewers check the finished job's changes and report only problems.
When
When a job's build is finished and its tests pass, before the pull request is opened.
Say it
run the lens inspection

Not forThe independent inspection (pack-review → xreview → adjudicate), not a review of an open PR (f-greploop).

It's working if
  • It stops and says so when foreman/jobs/ holds no job file for the slug, instead of reviewing anyway.
  • It stops and reports a wrong HEAD or an unexpected dirty file, and never checks out, stashes or resets anything.
  • It lists which lenses ran and which were skipped, each with a reason, and lens-simplify ran last as report-only.
  • Every finding carries severity, confidence, file:line, the scenario, why it matters and a concrete remedy.
  • Each lens is asked whether, where a test proves something is absent (no raw amount, no secret), another test pins the exact value shown.
  • A lens that halted, errored or returned BLOCK is reported as such, not as a clean lens with zero findings.
  • The findings are written to <day folder>/JOB-<N>-LENS-INSPECTION.md.
  • It gave no SHIP or BLOCK verdict, fixed nothing, and made no commit, push or PR.

pack-review

/pack-reviewskill
Full pagesample, questions, links→
Does
Bundle the goal, the rules and the changes into one brief for an outside reviewer.
When
Once the first review's fixes are in, to prepare the brief for the outside reviewer.

Not forNot a planning or build pack, and not a review (f-inspect, xreview).

It's working if
  • The output is one fenced block you can copy as it stands, with no commentary before or after it.
  • The brief has its sections in order: goal, hard constraints, non-goals, what to review, the diff or artifact, tests or repro, and what not to relitigate.
  • Nothing in the brief names a model or vendor: commit trailers and vendor-identifying paths are replaced, not summarised around.
  • Paths are absolute wherever the reviewer may run in a different checkout.
  • On a huge diff, it packs the file list and the riskiest hunks, and says what it left out.
  • It did not review the work, rewrite it, or edit any file.

xreview

/xreviewskill
Full pagesample, questions, links→
Does
Review another session's work with fresh eyes: list each claim, never rewrite it.
When
When the brief is ready and a session from a different AI company can review the work.

Not forOrdinary in-session code review (f-inspect, lens-code) and not for work this session wrote.

It's working if
  • It opens with an Independence statement: automated dispatch or hand-carried, and if hand-carried, whether it already knows the author or has seen the work.
  • It states the goal it reviewed against and says whether that goal was stated or inferred.
  • Every finding is a numbered row in the claim table (C1, C2 and so on), with a bucket, a severity, evidence and the smallest suggested change.
  • Anything it could not check is marked UNKNOWN, not promoted to a defect.
  • A clean review is a single C0 | No findings row naming what it actually read.
  • It ends after the six sections, with no essay, no rewrite and no file edits.
  • It refuses, and says why, when asked to review work this same session wrote or supervised.

adjudicate

/adjudicateskill
Full pagesample, questions, links→
Does
Go through a review point by point: accept, reject, partly accept, or can't verify.
When
When the outside reviewer's findings come back.

Not forReviewing (xreview, f-inspect) and not for a review this session wrote itself.

It's working if
  • It restates the original goal and hard constraints from this session before judging any claim.
  • It reads the reviewer's Independence statement first and says in one line if the reviewer was not blind.
  • Every claim gets one verdict, ACCEPT, REJECT, PARTIAL or CANNOT VERIFY, with evidence from the code, tests or spec.
  • There is no global agree or disagree.
  • It produces an apply list ordered blocker, major, minor, a reject list with the risk if wrong, and a needs-evidence list carrying the reviewer's questions verbatim.
  • With you present, it edits nothing until you answer: apply the accept list, apply a subset, or hold.

f-greploop

/f-greploopskill
Full pagesample, questions, links→
Does
Run an open pull request through Greptile's review, up to three rounds.
When
After the pull request is open, when you say "greploop".
Say it
mergegreploop

Not forThe lens inspection (f-inspect) or the independent inspection (xreview); never merges.

It's working if
  • It checks the PR's head matches the job's final head before asking for a review.
  • It asks with the comment @greptileai review exactly, once per round, and waits with the bundled greptile-wait.mjs, not a hand-rolled loop.
  • Every Greptile finding gets a recorded disposition, even on a 5/5 score.
  • Accepted findings go to the builder as test-first repair work orders; the foreman fixes nothing itself.

  • It stops after round 3 at the latest and hands over with each round's score, the open findings and the commits added.
  • The PR body is checked against the final diff before it tells you the PR is ready, and it never merges.

f-board

/f-boardskill
Full pagesample, questions, links→
Does
Update the project board when a job starts, merges, gets stuck or is added.
When
Whenever a job starts, merges, gets stuck or is added, so the board stays true.
Say it
gowhat's next?show me the board

Not forCutting a plan into jobs (f-schedule).

It's working if
  • Each event is one render_board.py command and bumps the board's version exactly once.
  • Ready and blocked are worked out by the tool, never stored, and only one job is in progress at a time.
  • The tool refuses bad data or an impossible event, such as starting a blocked job, closing without --next when a job becomes eligible, or a merged job without its SHA.

  • The builder is never named on the board.
  • Every job carries a summary that says in plain words what it builds, and check warns on a summary that is only plan references.

model-council

/model-councilskill
Full pagesample, questions, links→
Does
Ask a panel of AI models, each answering on its own, to help settle a design choice.
When
When a design choice is stuck between options and you've agreed to let AI models weigh in.

Not forPanels that must inspect the codebase (agent-council). Not for what tests or an evaluation should decide.

It's working if
  • Before any spending, it frames one neutral brief: a single decision question, two to four options, the constraints, and a request to choose and name the strongest argument against the choice. Its own lean is not in it.
  • It opens a one-shot picker for every run, where you choose 2 to 5 panel seats from different vendors and one auditor. It does not run without your pick.
  • The host's own model family never sits on the panel or as auditor, and when a Player is declared, that vendor's seat is dropped and the reduced count is stated.
  • Every seat gets the same scrubbed brief and sees nothing from the other seats, the chair or the repository in the first round.
  • The result is a decision the chair owns, with every first-round position, the convergence, the surviving dissent, and the auditor's verdict with the chair's answers.
  • It does not hand the decision back to you as a question, unless it needs an authority it does not hold, such as spending, publishing or deleting.
